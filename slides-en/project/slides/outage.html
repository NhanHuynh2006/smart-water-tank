05 · Reliability

Behaviour during a network outage

LOOP NEVER WAITS

Socket open capped at 0.5 s; nothing written to a socket that cannot take it, closed after 3 s. Longest loop period published as ctl_gap_ms.

NOTHING FORGOTTEN

240-sample ring buffer, 4 minutes at 1 Hz, replayed with "replay": true. Volume saved to flash every 60 s.

EVERYONE KNOWS

The broker publishes the last will after 1.5 × the 15 s keep alive (21.8 s measured); the dashboard turns red after 5 s.

FINDS ITS WAY BACK

Backoff from 1 to 30 s, a Wi-Fi join at most every 10 s, up to three networks, broker found by its mDNS name.

What happens when the network fails? Four mechanisms. First, the control loop never waits for the network. The lectures warn against connection logic inside a blocking loop. Opening a TCP socket to an unreachable broker still blocks inside the network stack, three seconds by default, so we cap it at half a second. Our outage test found a second trap: writing to a socket whose peer has silently vanished blocked the loop for up to ten seconds. Now the firmware checks, without waiting, that the socket can take data before every publish, and closes it after three seconds of refusals. The device publishes its longest loop period in the first message after reconnecting, so the claim can be checked: 0.88 seconds in our final test. Second, nothing is forgotten: while offline, telemetry goes into a ring buffer of 240 samples, four minutes at one hertz, replayed in order when the link returns. Volume is saved to flash every minute. Third, everyone knows: the broker publishes the last will after one and a half keep alive intervals, 21.8 seconds in the test, and the dashboard turns red after five seconds without data. Fourth, the device finds its way back: exponential backoff, a Wi-Fi join attempted at most every ten seconds, because restarting it every second never let it finish, up to three networks, and the broker found again by its mDNS name.
