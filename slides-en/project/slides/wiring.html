This slide matches Figures 2.4.1 and 2.4.2 of the report. On the left, every pin of the ESP32 and how its signal is conditioned. The ultrasonic echo is a five volt pulse, so it goes through a 10 and 20 kilo-ohm divider; the trigger can be driven directly. Both flow sensors are open collector outputs with 4.7 kilo-ohm pull ups to 3.3 volts, which removed the fifty hertz pickup we had with the weak internal pull ups. The current sensor output passes a one to one divider into GPIO 34, an input only pin. The relay moved to GPIO 10 on the fifth of October. That pin is normally wired to the flash chip, and it is free only when the flash runs in dual line DIO mode, so the build sets that mode; we checked on the bench that the relay then switches without resetting the chip. On the right, the power path. Twelve volts through a two ampere fuse into a five volt, five ampere buck converter. Every load leaves the converter terminal on its own wire, in a star. The pump path goes through the relay contact first, then the current sensor, then the pump, so the measured current is exactly the pump current. The pump carries a flyback diode and a 100 nanofarad capacitor at its terminals, and a 470 microfarad capacitor on the controller board buffers the inrush.
