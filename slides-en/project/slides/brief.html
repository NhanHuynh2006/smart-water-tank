The problem

A float valve answers one question. We need five.

Measure

Level and flow, with an error budget from a physical reference

Control

Keep the level in a band without the pump chattering

Protect

Never overflow, even with a broken sensor or a manual command

Diagnose

Tell a dry pump from a dead pump, spot abnormal consumption

Supervise

History, daily volume and alarms, viewable from anywhere

Survive

Keep controlling the tank when the network is gone

Project 08 · Smart Water Tank · 2

A mechanical float only stops the pump when the tank is full. It tells the owner nothing about consumption, leaks or a pump running dry. The assignment asks for five capabilities plus one overriding one: the tank must keep controlling itself during a network outage. That last requirement drives the whole architecture.
